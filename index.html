02 · Aboutanalyst summary
Analyst summaryfirst person

I work the defensive side of security engineering. Three years of it: two years running ArcSight and Sentinel across 5,000+ endpoints at Microland in Bengaluru, then a year as the primary cloud security advisor at Nurix Therapeutics, a public biotech, while finishing a master’s at Maryland.

Most of the recent work has been identity, because that is where the perimeter actually sits now. Twenty-plus Conditional Access policies across 600+ identities, Entra ID federated with AWS so one identity governs both clouds, and standing privilege eliminated through just-in-time elevation in PIM. The unglamorous part is that access controls fail by being routed around: a policy strict enough to look good on paper and awkward enough that someone files an exception is worse than no policy. So I design them against how people actually authenticate, then prove the posture with SOX access reviews rather than assuming it.

The other half is detection. Most of what a SIEM emits is noise, and tuning it down is slower and less celebrated than shipping a new rule, but it is the whole difference between a SOC that investigates and a SOC that has learned to ignore itself — which is why I care more about the 25% false-positive reduction at Nurix than about rule count, and why an ArcSight-to-Sentinel migration with no coverage gap is the Microland work I would defend first.

I spend time on the offensive side too — ROP chains, binary reverse engineering, C2 traffic reconstruction — not to pivot into red teaming, but because you cannot write credible detection for a technique you have never executed.

I am looking for full-time work in cloud security, detection engineering, or security architecture.
